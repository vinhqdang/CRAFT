\title{Anytime-Valid Conformal Monitoring for Weather-Onset Detection in Multimodal 3D Perception}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\institute{Anonymous Institution}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A perception stack that is accurate in clear weather offers no guarantee once snow begins to fall, and the transition itself is rarely instrumented: a 3D detector either keeps running silently degraded or fails outright, with no statistically principled signal marking the moment its outputs stopped being trustworthy. We wrap a multimodal 3D object detector's predictions in split conformal prediction, calibrated on held-out nominal-weather data, and track the resulting frame-level miscoverage rate with an anytime-valid sequential test built from testing-by-betting e-processes, so that a driving scene can be monitored continuously -- not at a fixed horizon, not with a post-hoc multiple-testing correction -- while still controlling the false-alarm rate under Ville's inequality. That backbone is not new; the closest predecessor already applies it, covariate-blind, to single-object 2D tracking failure. We extend it in two concrete ways: a betting rule conditioned on a cross-modal disagreement signal computed from the detector's own camera/LiDAR consistency probe, intended to act as a leading indicator of degradation rather than a lagging reaction to miscoverage alone; and a grid of per-BEV-cell e-processes with online false-discovery-rate control, designed to turn one global alarm into a live, spatially localized map of where the perception stack is currently untrustworthy -- implemented and validated end to end on the same real detector and dataset used for our global monitor, though not yet evaluated against a real multi-object scene with genuine spatial heterogeneity in where degradation occurs, which we flag as the natural next experiment rather than a result this paper already reports. We evaluate on Snowy Scenes, a real multimodal driving dataset with genuine snowfall rather than synthetic corruption, training the detector under an adversarial consistency objective and splicing held-out mild-snow frames with heavy-snowfall frames to construct a real, if assembled, onset transition. Getting a first real result exposed a failure mode worth reporting on its own terms: the covariate-informed and covariate-blind betting rules were statistically indistinguishable, traced to the detector's own consistency score collapsing to near-constant agreement under its training objective -- a lesson about reusing an internal robustness signal as an external monitoring covariate that we suspect generalizes beyond this one architecture. Fixing that objective and retraining did not, however, produce the result our method's rationale predicts: the corrected covariate now varies with real signal, but in the wrong direction for this task, and the covariate-informed bettor's operating curve is strictly worse than the covariate-blind baseline's, not better, on our real evaluation. We report both negative results plainly, trace the second to a plausible mechanism (adversarially-supervised disagreement transferring poorly to a naturally-occurring weather shift), and treat covariate-informed betting via this specific mechanism as an open problem this paper's evidence argues against rather than a validated contribution -- what we deliver instead is the real-data evaluation protocol, the spatial monitoring construction, and two documented, mechanistically explained failure modes that we believe are instructive independent of this paper's own outcome.

\end{abstract}
