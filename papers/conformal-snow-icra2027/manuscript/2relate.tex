\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Conformal Prediction and Its Sequential Extensions}

Split conformal prediction~\cite{VovkGammermanShafer2005,Lei2018JASA} calibrates a nonconformity score on a held-out set and produces prediction regions with finite-sample, distribution-free marginal coverage, without assumptions on the underlying model. That guarantee is exactly what makes it attractive for wrapping an otherwise unverifiable 3D detector: calibration.py in our implementation follows this standard recipe directly, restricting the nonconformity score to matched, ground-truth-occupied grid cells and taking the usual $\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil / n$ empirical quantile. None of this is novel, and we do not claim it is; it is the well-understood substrate the rest of the paper is built on.

The guarantee is a single-shot, batch guarantee, however, and applying it repeatedly to a stream introduces exactly the multiple-testing problem that anytime-valid inference exists to solve. Ville's inequality gives a nonnegative supermartingale a uniform bound on its running maximum, and testing by betting~\cite{WaudbySmithRamdas2024JRSSB,Ramdas2023GameTheoretic} recasts a sequential hypothesis test as choosing a betting fraction against that martingale structure: under the null, wealth cannot systematically grow, and the first time it crosses $1/\delta$ is a valid stopping rule at false-alarm level $\delta$, with no fixed monitoring horizon and no correction for how often the test is checked. This is the engine our method runs on (\texttt{betting.py}'s \texttt{WealthProcess}), and it is likewise an established result we apply rather than derive.

\subsection{Anytime-Valid Failure Detection for Streaming Vision}

The paper closest to ours, and the one we differentiate against explicitly throughout, is Monroy Mu\~noz, Verma, and Timans's sequential object-tracking-failure detector~\cite{MonroyMunoz2026WACV}. It frames tracking-quality monitoring as an e-process over a bounded per-frame metric $M_t$, with the betting rate $\lambda_t$ chosen adaptively from $M_t$'s own history via approximate GRAPA or Scale-Free Online Gradient Descent -- both of which we reproduce as covariate-blind baselines in \texttt{betting.py}'s \texttt{AGRAPABettor} and \texttt{SFOGDBettor} precisely so that our comparison is against their actual method, evaluated on our task, rather than against a method we invented to lose. Their evaluation spans four 2D visual-tracking benchmarks (OTB-100, LaSOT, TrackingNet, GOT-10k) and is explicitly single-object, per-video; the paper's own discussion lists multi-object extension as future work rather than a claim already made. Our covariate-informed betting rule and spatial multiplicity-control grid are direct responses to that stated gap, not an attempt to claim credit for the backbone they already built.

A handful of adjacent 2025--2026 papers touch related pieces without combining all of ours. Geng, Waite, Turnquist, Ivanov, and Ruchkin condition conformal error bounds on a robotic system's own dynamical state, combined with reachability analysis~\cite{Geng2025StateDependent}; this is covariate-conditioned like our approach, but batch conformal rather than sequential -- there is no e-process, no anytime-valid guarantee under repeated checking, and no weather or distribution-shift treatment. Kumar, Tayebati, Migliarba, Krishnan, and Trivedi learn context-conditioned nonconformity scores for robotic planning and perception from learned task representations~\cite{Kumar2025ContextAware}; again batch, not sequential, and the covariate is a learned embedding rather than a physically interpretable cross-modal disagreement signal. Liu et al.'s pre-fusion contextual calibration of multimodal signals~\cite{Liu2026ValueGated} uses cross-modal agreement and conflict to modulate fusion, which is architecturally close to what our monitored detector's own consistency probe already does internally, but purely as a learned neural gate rather than as a covariate feeding a statistical test, and targets audio-visual and sentiment tasks rather than 3D driving perception. Antonante, Spivak, and Carlone's foundational perception-monitoring work~\cite{Antonante2020Monitoring} frames monitoring and diagnosability graph-theoretically; it is the right citation for the general framing of "monitoring perception systems" at a robotics venue, but is not a competing statistical method. None of these four combines (a) anytime-valid sequential testing, (b) multi-object spatial resolution with multiplicity control, and (c) real adverse-weather distribution shift in multimodal 3D driving perception; \cite{MonroyMunoz2026WACV} is the one that combines the first without the other two, which is why it is the baseline we beat rather than merely cite. We also explored conditioning the betting rate on an external covariate -- the same disagreement signal Liu et al.'s gate and our monitored detector's own consistency probe both compute internally -- as a fourth possible axis of contribution; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports why that specific attempt did not hold up on real data, and we do not count it among the differentiators above.

\subsection{Online Multiplicity Control}

Testing many simultaneous hypotheses -- here, many BEV grid cells at once, every frame -- without inflating the false-discovery rate is itself a studied problem once the test statistics are e-values rather than $p$-values. Wang and Ramdas's e-BH procedure~\cite{WangRamdas2022JRSSB} rejects the hypotheses whose e-values exceed a data-dependent threshold determined by the largest $k$ such that the $k$-th order statistic clears $n/(kq)$, and controls FDR at level $q$ under arbitrary dependence across the e-values -- an important property here, since neighboring BEV cells' wealth processes are certainly not independent. \texttt{spatial.py} implements this procedure directly (\texttt{e\_bh\_reject}) alongside a simpler Bonferroni per-cell budget as a conservative baseline, matching the two multiplicity-control regimes plan.md lists as baselines to beat: Bonferroni is valid but conservative (higher detection latency), while e-BH is the online, less conservative alternative our spatial grid is built to exploit.

\subsection{Adverse-Weather Perception Data and Multimodal Fusion}

Snow-specific autonomous-driving datasets exist -- the Canadian Adverse Driving Conditions dataset~\cite{Pitropov2021CADC} being the most widely used prior example, and Bijelic et al.'s fog- and rain-focused multimodal fusion work~\cite{Bijelic2020SeeingThroughFog} addressing an adjacent adverse-weather regime -- but neither is built around a real, in-dataset clear-to-snow onset transition, and neither is designed to support the calibration/monitoring split our method needs. Snowy Scenes~\cite{SnowyScenes2026}, described fully in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, is a substantially more recent (2026) multimodal dataset captured entirely in real snowy conditions; the price of that specificity, as we found once real access arrived, is that it contains no clear-weather split at all, which we address by measuring a real, physically grounded severity ordering across its own weather categories rather than assuming the dataset would hand us the clear/snow contrast we had originally planned around. Our monitored detector, CRAF-X, is described in a companion submission~\cite{CRAFXTechReport2026}: it fuses camera and LiDAR BEV features through a Cross-modal Consistency Probe (CCP) trained to detect geometric and semantic disagreement between modalities, which is the same signal this paper repurposes as the covariate for covariate-informed betting -- Section~\ref{sec:method} describes exactly what that probe computes and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} describes what happened when its training objective did not, initially, produce a covariate with any real signal in it at all.
