\begin{highlights}
\item A null-detector diagnostic exposes silent failure in conformal perception monitors
\item Zeroing the detector reproduces monitor output bit-for-bit under score collapse
\item Cross-session exchangeability failure inflates miscoverage under pooled calibration
\item Session-conditional calibration gives inconsistent gains at this sample size
\item A drive-disjoint weather-effect estimate is an underpowered null, not an absence
\end{highlights}

\begin{abstract}
Autonomous navigation stacks increasingly monitor perception with conformal
prediction: calibrate a nonconformity score, track its miscoverage with an
anytime-valid sequential test, and alarm when perception becomes
untrustworthy. We set out to reduce detection delay for such a monitor on
camera/LiDAR 3D detection under real adverse weather; we did not, and
report two silent failure modes instead. First, when a detector's
regression head degenerates, its nonconformity score reduces to a function
of ground-truth labels alone, and every downstream component --
calibration, sequential betting, alarm timing -- behaves exactly as
specified while measuring nothing about perception. We introduce a
null-detector diagnostic, a one-pass control that reruns the pipeline with
the detector's output zeroed, and show it reproduces our own earlier
results bit-for-bit. Second, split conformal's exchangeability assumption
fails across driving sessions on the Canadian Adverse Driving Conditions
dataset: per-drive miscoverage under one pooled quantile varies several-fold
across drives that are all nominal, with between-session variation
comparable to the weather effect sought, so the guarantee remains formally
correct while becoming operationally useless -- the monitor tracks route
identity, not perception loss. On the weather effect itself we report an
underpowered null, not a demonstrated absence, and show why an earlier,
in-sample version of the same measurement had appeared significant. We
recommend both the null-detector diagnostic and session-conditional
evaluation as standard practice for conformal perception monitoring in
navigation.
\end{abstract}
