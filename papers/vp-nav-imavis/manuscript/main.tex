\begin{abstract}
Conformal prediction combined with anytime-valid sequential testing offers a
principled way to monitor a deployed perception stack: calibrate a
nonconformity score on nominal data, track its miscoverage on the live
stream, and alarm when a betting process accumulates enough evidence that
the nominal assumption has failed. We set out to apply this recipe to
multimodal 3D detection under real adverse weather and to improve its
detection delay. We report instead three findings we believe are more
useful, and a plain negative result on the original goal.

First, a monitor of this kind can produce entirely plausible numbers while
measuring nothing about the detector. We introduce a \emph{null-detector
diagnostic} --- rerun the pipeline with the detector's regression head
output replaced by zeros --- which costs no additional data, training or
annotation. On two real adverse-weather datasets it reproduced our own
previously reported detection delays \emph{exactly}, to the frame, from a
model with no functioning detection head, because a collapsed detector
reduces the nonconformity score to a function of the ground-truth labels
alone. We recommend this control as standard practice.

Second, split conformal's exchangeability assumption fails across driving
sessions. On the Canadian Adverse Driving Conditions dataset we measure a
$7.5\times$ spread in per-drive miscoverage under a single pooled quantile
($0.083$ to $0.623$ against a target of $0.20$), with between-session
standard deviation as large as the weather effect itself. Mondrian
session-conditional calibration repairs this on a mid-quality checkpoint
(effect-to-noise $0.55 \rightarrow 0.85$; worst-case per-session deviation
$0.41 \rightarrow 0.19$), but that advantage \emph{reverses} on a converged
checkpoint, so the benefit is model-dependent rather than unconditional.

Third, and most consequentially, monitorability is \emph{non-monotone in
detector quality}. Across five trained checkpoints spanning a real quality
range, the measured weather effect declines monotonically as the detector
improves ($0.108 \rightarrow 0.007$ for a phantom-detection score) and
becomes statistically undetectable beyond a heatmap separation of roughly
$0.41$. Two structurally different nonconformity scores --- one blind to
phantom detections, one dominated by them --- agree on this. The practical
warning is uncomfortable: \emph{improving your detector can silently
disable your monitor}, because a detector that degrades gracefully leaves
no error differential to detect, and the monitor keeps emitting plausible
numbers throughout.

We are explicit about power: only two of six curve points reach
significance with $18$ sessions, so the claim rests on the monotone
effect-size trend and the loss of detectability, not on a series of
significant measurements. On the original objective we are equally plain
--- at the effect sizes available here reliable detection is not
achievable, and we report that with intervals rather than burying it.
\end{abstract}
