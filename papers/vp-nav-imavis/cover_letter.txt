Cover Letter
Submission to: Image and Vision Computing, Special Issue "Visual Perception enabling
Autonomous Navigation (VP-NAV)"
Article type: VSI: VP-NAV

Dear Guest Editors,

We submit our manuscript, "From Robust Fusion to Statistically Valid Monitoring: An
Anytime-Valid Conformal Layer for Detecting Real-World Weather-Onset Degradation in
Multimodal 3D Perception," for consideration in the VP-NAV special issue.

Disclosure of a related manuscript under review elsewhere

In the interest of full transparency, we disclose that a related manuscript, describing
a multimodal (camera/LiDAR) fusion detector -- the Cross-modal Consistency Probe (CCP),
Gated Adaptive Fusion Module (GAFM), and Adversarial Consistency Training (ACT) objective
summarized as background in Section 3.1 of this submission -- is currently under review
at The Visual Computer (Springer), and is not yet accepted or published.

This submission is not a duplicate or overlapping submission of that manuscript's
contribution. The companion manuscript's own contribution is the detector architecture
itself and its evaluation against adversarial and sensor-degradation scenarios; this
submission neither restates nor re-derives that manuscript's own reported experimental
results, and treats the detector strictly as background infrastructure, described only
to the depth needed to make this submission self-contained for a reader unfamiliar with
the companion work. This submission's own, independent contribution is a statistically
principled monitoring layer built on top of that detector's outputs -- anytime-valid
sequential testing via testing-by-betting e-processes, extended to spatially-resolved
per-BEV-cell monitoring with online false-discovery-rate control -- together with its
evaluation against real adverse-weather driving data from two independent datasets
(Snowy Scenes and the Canadian Adverse Driving Conditions dataset), and an honestly
reported negative finding concerning one candidate monitoring covariate (Section 4.1.1
and Section 5.2), none of which appear in the companion manuscript.

We are prepared to provide the companion manuscript, its current review status, or any
further clarification the editors require to assess the relationship between the two
submissions.

We believe this work aligns closely with the special issue's stated scope: multi-modal
sensor fusion for autonomous navigation, learning-based perception systems, and robust
visual perception under adverse weather conditions, validated with real benchmark
datasets rather than only synthetic corruption.
